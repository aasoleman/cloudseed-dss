CloudSeed DSS — Physics-Informed RL Decision Support for Cloud Seeding

Research prototype of the R-PIBRL framework · Hybrid Intelligent Predictive Models for Evaluating Cloud Seeding Impact

Prepared by: Alaa Alarood · University of Jeddah

Research problem

Arid regions such as the UAE and Saudi Arabia rely on cloud seeding to enhance rainfall, yet its effectiveness is hard to prove and hard to time: the signal is small against natural variability, and purely data-driven models ignore atmospheric physics. This prototype demonstrates an end-to-end decision-support pipeline that (1) detects seeding-related change in rainfall against a control region, (2) learns rainfall from physically constrained models, and (3) recommends when seeding is worth its cost.

1 · Data7 stations, daily 2000–2025 (Meteostat-style schema)
2 · FeaturesTarget-Control Regression, CUSUM, Mann-Kendall change point
3 · PhysicsKoren-Feingold aerosol–cloud–precipitation prior φ(A,C,W)
4 · Bellman RLQ-learning Seed / NoSeed, physics-initialised
5 · DecisionWhat-if console with recommendation and confidence

Objectives

  • Quantify seeding-related shifts in precipitation using target/control regression and non-parametric change-point tests.
  • Embed aerosol–cloud–precipitation physics in learning (soft physics loss + physics-initialised Bellman agent).
  • Compare against a data-only GBDT baseline and rule-of-thumb seeding policies with transparent metrics.
  • Provide an interactive what-if tool for operational planners.

National priority

Water security is a national priority (Saudi Vision 2030 and the National Water Strategy 2030). Rain enhancement is an active programme in the region; evidence-based evaluation and timing reduce wasted operations and strengthen the scientific case for seeding.

Scope: this is a research prototype running on synthetic UAE-like data (or a CSV you upload). It is not an operational forecasting or seeding system.

What-if decision console

Set the current atmospheric state. The trained physics-informed network estimates rainfall; the Bellman agent compares Seed vs NoSeed. Confidence depends on how often similar states were seen in training.

Answer to the review-form question: what research problem does the prototype solve?

It provides a reproducible, physics-aware way to (a) detect and quantify cloud-seeding-related change in rainfall against a control region, and (b) decide, state by state, when seeding is expected to pay off, instead of relying on fixed rules or purely statistical post-event assessment.
